Selected work

2026 Center for Security and Emerging Technology Semester Researcher, ETO data team · Georgetown University, Washington, DC

Scout: three hundred articles in, thirty out

Scout is ETO's tracker for Chinese tech news. Analysts used to read about 300 articles a day to find the ones that mattered. Using AI coding tools, I built a Python pipeline with an LLM API to screen the articles, narrowing about 300 to roughly 30 for human review.

The prompts were tuned through rounds of human spot-checks. The pipeline passed analyst code review, and I was asked to share the method with the team behind Scout's autotagging.

Python / Anthropic SDK / pyairtable / human-in-the-loop evaluation
2026 Center for Security and Emerging Technology Sole author, two internal research reports

Where the chokepoint moves, and where safety tests stop

Photoresist. I went through filings and industry research on 20 Chinese firms across technology tiers. Capacity is growing, but advanced-node resists still trail foreign suppliers, and the inputs underneath them, resins and photoinitiators, are still imported.

Open-weight safety. Open-weight models are safety-tested with guardrails on, yet routine fine-tuning strips them. I reviewed 10+ papers, compared four third-party evaluations of Kimi K3 and GLM-5.2, and proposed testing the stripped and maliciously fine-tuned versions too.

2025 Carnegie Mellon, Summer Undergraduate Research Research Assistant to Prof. Justin Canfil · Remote

A treaty database, coded by hand

I coded more than 350 international treaties on my own, on a 14-point institutional framework: dispute settlement, verification, amendment rules, standing agencies, entry into force.

Many entries arrived as only a name or a link, so I tracked down the original texts, mostly through the UN Treaty Collection, and filled in status and membership. I collected this data for my professor's research on AI governance.

Institutional coding / primary sources / comparative design
2025 UCloud AI Research & Development Intern · Shanghai

Agents that argue before they ship

With AI coding assistance, I implemented two multi-agent software-design frameworks, KGMAF and NTU's MAAD, on MetaGPT. Neither paper released code, so the implementations followed the designs described in the papers.

Then I added a second reviewer that debates the first. Work moves on only when both agree; if they still disagree after three rounds, a human decides. Model-rated confidence rose, cost rose at least threefold, and one or two runs in five ended with a person. I wrote it up as a governance framework and pitched it to the department lead.

Python / MetaGPT / multi-agent evaluation
